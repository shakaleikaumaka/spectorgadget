Your vacancy-filling agent, in your pocket.

Spector Gadget is the planned iOS + Android app for Spector — the tenant-prospecting engine that works a commercial vacancy every business day until it fills, then ends its own subscription in celebration. This paper describes what the broker holds in their hand: the mission dashboard, the daily lead feed, one-tap approve-and-send outreach, and the Vacancy Filled Receipt.

See the screens How the engine works
Abstract

The one-paragraph version

The problem: a vacant commercial suite costs its landlord rent every day, and the listing broker's prospecting time is the scarcest input in filling it. Most vacancies are marketed passively — a flyer, a LoopNet entry, and hope.

The engine (live today): Spector is an AI agent that actively prospects a listed vacancy every business day: rotating through news, franchise-development wires, and ground-truth sources; deduplicating and qualifying named candidate tenants with a reason and a public source for each; and drafting personalized outreach that the broker approves and sends. It runs until the vacancy fills — then the subscription ends itself and mints a commemorative Vacancy Filled Receipt.

This paper (the app): today that engine speaks email. Spector Gadget wraps it in a phone-native client — because brokers live on their phones, between showings — turning the daily loop into a lead feed, the approval into one tap, and a prospect's reply into a push notification the broker can act on in the parking lot.

Section 1

The vacancy problem

Every listing broker knows this math. Few have a tool aimed squarely at it.

~$4,500/mo

Carrying cost of one empty suite

A 2,700 SF retail suite at ~$20/SF NNN is roughly \$4,500/month of rent not being collected — every month it sits. (Illustrative; your suite's number is on your own flyer.)

Minutes/day

What prospecting actually gets

Brokers juggle many listings; proactive tenant-hunting for any one of them happens in stolen minutes. Signals — a chain signing a franchise deal, a food truck telling a reporter it wants a brick-and-mortar — scroll past unseen.

96.3%

Where the value should stay

The commission on a filled lease is thousands of dollars. A prospecting tool should cost a rounding error of that and leave the relationship — and the win — with the broker. That ratio is Spector's design constraint, not a marketing line.

Section 2

The vision: a day with Spector in your pocket

What the app feels like to a working broker, from listing to lease. Everything below maps to engine behavior that exists today (see §3) — the app changes the surface, not the substance.

Day 0 · 4 minutes

Put a listing on the Engine

Snap or paste the flyer, confirm address and suites, note any deal facts (NNN vs gross, restrictions, "never pitch competitors of my anchor"). Subscription starts: \$36.90/mo for this property. The meter is mission-shaped — it stops the day the space fills.

Day 1–2

The onboarding report lands

A full prospecting report: trade-area facts with sources, a tenant-fit thesis, 15–25 named candidate tenants ranked by likelihood-to-transact — each with a one-line reason, a public source link, and a contact path. Readable in the app, forwardable as a branded PDF.

Every business day

The engine runs; the feed updates

Spector rotates sources daily — business journals, franchise wires, ground truth. New qualified leads appear in the feed with their reason and source. Quiet days say so honestly: "3 sources checked, no new candidates, list still fresh."

Whenever a draft is ready

One tap: approve → send

Tier-1 leads come with a personalized outreach draft. The broker reads it, edits if they like, and taps Approve & Send — the note goes out from the broker's own identity. Spector proposes; the human sends. Always.

Monday, 90 seconds

The digest

New leads this week, the re-ranked call-first five, drafts awaiting a tap, movement (replies, retirements with comp intel), and what Spector needs from you. One screen, no filler.

The moment it matters

🔔 "A prospect replied"

A push notification with the reply summarized, a suggested response drafted, and the prospect's context card — actionable from a parking lot between showings. Speed-to-response is where deals are won.

Day it fills 🎉

The celebration — and the receipt

Tap Mark as FILLED. Billing stops that day. Spector mints the Vacancy Filled Receipt — a branded certificate: engine start, lease date, days-to-filled, the journey. The only subscription that celebrates its own cancellation.

Section 3

How the engine works Live today

This is the honest mechanics — not app fiction. The engine operates now over email; the app is a thin client over exactly this loop.

1

Inbox & instruction check

Every pass starts by reading what changed: broker instructions, new deal facts, prospect replies, a suite leasing. A leased suite retires every lead sized to it — the thesis re-ranks before any new prospecting.

2

Source rotation

Different source families each weekday — local business press, food & retail media, franchise-development wires and state feeds, brand location-page truth checks, and a Friday ground-truth sweep of the trade area. Everything gets covered weekly without hammering anything daily; every scan is logged.

3

Dedup → qualify

Every surfaced business is canonicalized and checked against the property's lead ledger. Known lead → signal update, not a duplicate. New candidate → the full rubric: expansion-intent tier, footprint vs. available suites, co-tenancy conflicts, economics sanity, recency, callability. Rejections are kept, with reasons, so nothing is re-researched.

4

Draft → propose → human sends

Qualified tier-1/2 leads get a personalized draft: a hook proving homework was done, one sentence of numbers that matter to their model, an honest opt-out. The broker approves and it goes out under the broker's identity. Spector cannot send prospect mail — structurally, not just by policy.

The mission shape

Every pass opens by incrementing the day counter and asking one question: what gets this vacancy filled faster today? The loop's success condition is its own termination. When the broker confirms the lease, billing stops, the receipt is minted, the workspace is archived intact for the client, and days-to-filled joins Spector's public scoreboard — which is only ever updated with real missions, including the ones that end without a fill.

Section 6

The covenant & the price

The economics are part of the product design, stated plainly.

\$36.90 per month, per property — until it fills. One property, up to ~4 suites, one trade area. The subscription is mission-shaped: it is supposed to end, and its natural ending is a celebration, not churn. When the broker marks the vacancy FILLED, billing stops that day and the record converts into the commemorative receipt.

96.3% of the value stays with the broker. A \$36.90/mo tool that helps land a lease worth a five-figure commission keeps essentially all of the win — and the relationship — with the human who made the call. Spector's split: 0.33 → founder, 3.36 → company/compute, 96.3 → the client's side of the table.

Propose, never send. Every prospect-facing message is drafted by Spector and sent by a human, under the human's identity. In v1 this is structural — Spector has no path to send prospect mail at all.

Public data + honest outreach only. No scraping behind logins, no fake identities, no spam. Every lead carries a public source link; unverifiable numbers are labeled as assumptions, never asserted. Sources that block bots are noted, not circumvented dishonestly.

The guarantee. If the onboarding report doesn't genuinely help, the month is refunded. The scoreboard reports all missions — including the ones that end unfilled.

Section 7

Technology & honesty notes

A white paper should say what's real. Here's exactly what is.

What Spector is. Spector is a persistent AI agent running on the Taurus multi-agent platform — durable infrastructure with its own workspace, memory, and scheduled runs. The prospecting engine (daily source rotation, lead ledger with dedup, qualification rubric, outreach queue, digest, filled-flow) is specified and operating today, delivering over email and shared documents. Real reports for real Colorado listings have shipped.

What the app is. Spector Gadget is a thin client: the phone renders state the agent already maintains (missions, ledgers, queues, digests) and sends back the broker's decisions (approve, decline, facts, FILLED). No intelligence moves into the phone; the app is a window, which is why the email-first engine and the app can honestly coexist — same loop, two surfaces.

What is live vs. roadmap. Live the engine, the reports, the pricing covenant, the storefront at spectoragent.com. Roadmap everything in Section 4–5: the native apps, push notifications, in-app approve, team seats. Scoreboard numbers shown in mockups are illustrative; the public scoreboard launches empty and fills only with real missions. We will not claim an average days-to-filled we haven't earned.

Data handling. Broker-provided deal facts stay in the mission workspace, delivered to the broker; they are not resold or shared across clients. Lead research uses public sources only.
